# Market Research Summary

Summary of the [Perplexity Report (original)](perplexity-report.md) plus Claude's review of it. Player numbers and links in the report were not verified.

## Starting point

Steal An Egg reached about 1.6M concurrent players. Simple game, high numbers, strong power-boost feeling. Many rebrands exist: Steal a Brainrot, Steal an Anime, Steal a Fish, Steal a Pet, Steal a Meme, and many more.

## The two formulas

1. **Conveyor-base (Steal a Brainrot):** units walk down a shared lane, you buy them, they earn money at your base, others sneak in and steal them, base shield on a timer, rebirth.
2. **Guardian-fetch (Steal An Egg):** grab an egg from a guarded zone, escape the guardian, carry it home, hatch it into an earner, train speed, unlock harder zones.

Our game uses **guardian-fetch**, with boss fights as the guardian.

## Already taken (relevant to us)

- Steal an Anime, Steal Anime, Steal an Anime Egg: so anime alone isn't new. Our difference is the boss fights, stands and abilities from boss copies.

## What the report got right

- Most clones are the same template with new models and names.
- The server must control cash, inventory, steals and income; exploiters target these games.
- Fair loss rules keep players: new-player protection, no stealing from offline players.
- Don't use leaked or "uncopylocked" games; check every script in any kit.
- Competitor revenue estimates ($3M–15M/month) are guesses.

## Where Claude disagreed

- The report's "Spell Heist" pick was too complex for the audience and spellbooks are a weak collectible. Rejected; see [Decisions Log](../planning/decisions.md#2-spell-heist-rejected).
- It underrated what actually grows these games: like goals, codes, weekly admin events, YouTubers, thumbnails and ads. See [Spectacle and YouTube](../design/spectacle-and-youtube.md).
- Its 8-week MVP was too big for a solo beginner.
