# Roblox “Steal A…” Market Map and Original Game Plan

## Executive answer

The market is much larger than a few visible hits. Search results confirm dozens of active, inactive, duplicate, localized, modded, and derivative experiences, but Roblox does not expose a reliable finite catalog of “all” experiences through ordinary web search. The useful conclusion is not that every noun is taken; it is that most competitors belong to just **two reusable game architectures**, and many merely replace the collectible models and text.

The best opening is therefore **not an unused noun alone**. It is an original collectible fantasy paired with a theft mechanic that changes how players move, choose targets, defend, and socialize. For a solo software builder, the strongest direction found in this research is **Steal a Spell**—or the more distinctive title **Spell Heist**—where collected spells are both passive earners and usable abilities.

## Scope and limitations

This is a best-effort market map compiled from Roblox listings, Rolimon’s pages, and current web indexing in October 2026. It includes meaningful named variants and representative duplicates, but cannot truthfully claim every published “Steal A…” place: Roblox has millions of experiences, search ranking is personalized and dynamic, duplicate places disappear or are renamed, and some pages are not indexed.

Live player counts also change minute by minute. Visit totals and player figures from third-party pages should be treated as snapshots or estimates, not audited business results. In particular, third-party revenue estimates for Steal An Egg vary dramatically and are not developer disclosures, so no precise earnings claim is justified.[^1][^2]

## The two formulas

### Conveyor-base formula

This is the Steal a Brainrot pattern:

1. Eight or so players receive plots around a shared lane.
2. Collectible units appear on a conveyor, portal, tunnel, road, river, or runway.
3. A player buys a unit; it walks or moves to the player’s plot.
4. Placed units generate cash per second.
5. Rivals enter an unlocked plot, hold an interaction prompt, carry one unit home, and become vulnerable while escaping.
6. Owners use a timed base shield, slap tools, traps, or gadgets.
7. Higher rarity, mutations, events, and rebirth expand the exponential economy.

Steal a Brainrot’s official listing describes buying a unit, stealing from players, generating money, rebirth, and troll gear. Steal a Fish adds the operational details: fish travel down a shared route, occupy base slots, earn cash, can be protected by a timed force field, and rebirth improves the shield and income. An open-source work-in-progress template exposes the same modular decomposition: plots, a shared item-spawning area, passive income, stealing, and rebirth.[^3][^4][^5]

### Guardian-fetch formula

This is the newer Steal An Egg pattern:

1. Each player owns a pen/base and starts near a low-risk biome.
2. Valuable objects sit inside guarded zones.
3. Taking one triggers an NPC chase or environmental danger.
4. The player must physically carry it home; getting caught or hit drops it.
5. At home it hatches, opens, or transforms into a passive-income collectible.
6. Income upgrades movement/training and base capacity, opening harder zones.
7. Size, rarity, mutations, reset events, and contested rare spawns generate collection depth.

The official description reduces this to eggs → pets → income → treadmill/base upgrades → Speed → stealing from players. Detailed guides report biome guardians as speed checks and describe weight, rarity, PvP tools, and timed egg resets. This formula has already been reskinned into anime eggs, pets rescued from animal bosses, plushies guarded by monsters, and vehicle/car variants.[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10][^11]

## Why simple works

The loop is understandable from a thumbnail and one sentence. Acquisition, ownership, passive growth, theft risk, and visible comparison all occur in the same physical space. Every collectible is simultaneously:

- A trophy
- An income generator
- A theft target
- A status signal
- A reason to attack or defend
- A future update slot

The “high numbers” feeling is only one layer. The more important psychological structure is **loss aversion plus public status**: a rare unit is visible to other players and can disappear, so passive farming becomes socially tense. The steal action also creates user-generated stories without requiring authored quests.

Steal An Egg improves the original architecture by making acquisition itself active. Guardian chases, carry weight, routes, dropped loot, and biome thresholds turn what could be an AFK tycoon into a short risk/reward run. Its two reported permanent passes—double money and double growth—accelerate the loop without making core areas or pets exclusively inaccessible.[^9][^54][^55]

## How clones are built

The similarity is not accidental. Public and commercial starter kits advertise the exact reusable systems: player plots, item spawning, passive income, stealing, rebirth, DataStore saving, shops and monetization. Roblox Studio projects are commonly shipped as `.rbxl`/`.rbxm` templates, letting a developer replace models, names, rarity tables, UI colors and thumbnails while retaining most server systems.[^56][^5][^57]

A clean implementation would normally contain these server-authoritative modules:

| Module | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| PlotService | Assign/release plots, track owner, shield state, slot capacity |
| CollectibleCatalog | ID, display name, rarity, cost, income, weight, model asset, mutation pool |
| SpawnService | Weighted spawning, lane movement or guarded-zone placement, event injections |
| PurchaseService | Validate balance and availability on the server; reserve a unit atomically |
| CarryService | Attach item to character, apply movement penalty, drop on hit/death/leave |
| StealService | Validate victim plot, shield, distance, prompt duration, destination and cooldown |
| IncomeService | Aggregate placed-unit income and apply verified multipliers |
| ProgressionService | Upgrades, zone requirements, rebirth/reset and permanent bonuses |
| SaveService | Profile/session locking, schema migrations, retries, receipts and offline timestamps |
| EventService | Rare-spawn countdowns, weather/mutations, live-event modifiers |
| MonetizationService | Pass ownership, developer-product receipts, starter offers and boosts |
| AnalyticsService | Tutorial funnel, first acquisition, first steal, loss events, upgrade choices and purchase funnel |

The client should request actions and render feedback, but the server must own cash, inventory, spawn claims, steals, income, purchases and movement sanity checks. Public exploit scripts explicitly target instant interaction, teleport-to-base, fly/no-clip and automatic prompt activation, showing exactly why client trust would be unsafe.[^58]

## Product whitespace

The searches found direct competition for anime, pets, fish, memes, YouTubers, games, countries, vehicles, planes, dragons, monsters, plushies, squishies, glitches, weapons, eggs, and even skills/magic. Broadly generic “magic” is therefore not completely unused.[^45][^46]

However, **a polished spell-heist game with combinable, usable spells and guardian vaults** was not found in this search. The distinction matters: “Steal a Skill” treats powers as roaming purchasable units, while the proposed concept treats every collectible as an economic asset, a theft tool, and a recipe component.

Other comparatively open directions found in exact-title checks include:

| Concept | Why it can work | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Steal a Spell / Spell Heist** | Immediate fantasy; spells can be used, fused and countered; original assets are feasible | “Steal a Skill/Magic” exists, so differentiation must be mechanical and visual |
| **Steal a Storm** | Storms can alter the whole server—rain, lightning, tornado, blizzard—making collectibles feel alive | Harder VFX and performance budget |
| **Steal a Shadow** | A player’s shadow can fight, earn, disguise, or copy a power; strong thumbnail identity | Requires especially clear onboarding |
| **Steal a Constellation** | Collect stars from celestial guardians and connect sets; visually distinctive and globally readable | Abstract objects may feel less emotionally collectible |
| **Steal a Curse** | Curses can generate money but also harmful side effects, creating meaningful trade-offs | Avoid anime franchise references and overly dark imagery |
| **Steal a Secret** | Secrets produce “influence”; stealing involves disguise, deduction and social bluffing | Less instantly legible to young players |

“Not found” means no strong indexed exact-title competitor appeared during this research; it is not a trademark clearance or a guarantee that no small Roblox place exists.

## Recommended concept

### Steal a Spell / Spell Heist

**One-line pitch:** Raid elemental vaults, escape their guardians, place living spellbooks in your tower to generate Mana, and use those same spells to steal or defend.

### Core loop

1. Start with a one-floor wizard tower and a weak Dash spell.
2. Enter the Meadow Vault and steal a spell orb from a sleeping or patrolling guardian.
3. Escape while carrying it; taking damage drops the orb for anyone.
4. Bring it to a pedestal, where it opens into a spellbook.
5. The spellbook generates Mana per second.
6. Equip one spell from the collection for active use.
7. Spend Mana on movement training, tower slots, shield runes and access to harder vaults.
8. Raid players’ towers when their ward expires, then escape with one spellbook.
9. Fuse duplicate spell families into reactions and rebirth as an Archmage for permanent bonuses.

### The differentiator

In most clones, the collectible only changes its model and income number. Here every spell changes play:

- Wind: dash or push a carrier
- Ice: freeze a floor temporarily
- Earth: create a short wall
- Blink: teleport a short validated distance
- Decoy: create a fake valuable spellbook
- Reveal: expose traps and disguised books
- Swap: exchange positions with a marked decoy
- Ward: shorten one incoming disable

This makes collection strategy more important than simply owning the highest income unit. A lower-income Blink or Reveal can be more valuable for a robbery loadout than another top earner.

### Fusion reactions

Duplicate collection becomes useful instead of disappointing:

- Fire + Wind → Firestorm
- Water + Ice → Blizzard
- Earth + Lightning → Magnetism
- Light + Shadow → Eclipse
- Time + Space → Rift

The reaction should alter appearance, income and one ability property. Keep launch scope to four elements and six reactions; do not build twenty systems before validating the theft loop.

### Social mechanics

- **Revenge contract:** A player who steals from you becomes marked; recovering any item from that player grants a temporary bonus.
- **False pedestal:** One slot can display a fake high-rarity book, creating bluffing.
- **Duel circle:** Voluntary risk-free testing of equipped spells.
- **Party ritual:** Three players channel different elements to open a timed world vault.
- **Server prophecy:** A visible countdown predicts the next rare family but not its exact vault.

These features turn co-play into a system rather than only an invite bonus. Roblox currently emphasizes play days, playtime, intentional co-play, spend days and long-term value in Recommendations.[^59][^60]

## MVP scope

A sensible eight-week solo/small-team MVP is:

| Area | Launch scope |
|---|---|
| Server | 8 players, 8 radial plots |
| World | Hub plus 4 vault biomes |
| Collection | 36 spellbooks: 6 common, 8 uncommon, 8 rare, 6 epic, 4 legendary, 4 mythic |
| Variation | 4 sizes, 5 mutations, no trading at launch |
| Abilities | Dash, Push, Ice Patch, Wall, Decoy, Reveal |
| Progression | 10 tower upgrades, 10 movement tiers, 5 rebirth levels |
| Events | Rare vault every 5 minutes; one weekend mutation event |
| Monetization | 2 permanent passes, 3 repeatable products, cosmetic trail/aura shop |
| Content cadence | 2 spells weekly; one reaction or event every 2 weeks |

### Build order

1. Graybox eight plots, one vault, one guardian, one carry/drop flow.
2. Add a single spellbook that earns Mana and saves reliably.
3. Implement player theft, ward timing, hit/drop and safe delivery.
4. Instrument the tutorial and first-session funnel.
5. Add upgrades and only enough content to test a 20-minute session.
6. Run closed tests focused on confusion, griefing and exploit attempts.
7. Add rarity/mutations and additional vaults after the core loop retains testers.
8. Add monetization last, at natural acceleration moments.

Roblox’s own current advice is to fix core gameplay and retention before pushing monetization; it highlights a clear first session, satisfying loop, return reasons, and fair offers connected to player goals.[^61]

## Economy blueprint

Use a geometric curve but hide the formula behind rounded, readable values:

- Unit purchase/value curve: each tier approximately 2.0–2.5× the previous tier.
- Mana-per-second curve: approximately 1.7–2.0× per tier.
- Movement requirement: slower than income growth so players feel increasingly powerful.
- Tower slots: expensive enough to force replacement decisions, not so expensive that early players wait idle.
- Rebirth: available after roughly 25–40 minutes for an engaged first-time player, then progressively longer unless mastery improves.

Do not begin with quadrillions. Start at 1 Mana/s and let the first session reach thousands or low millions; reserve gigantic suffixes for long-term escalation. Show increases with animations, sound, physical tower growth and access—not only extra zeroes.

### Fair loss rules

Theft is the content, but uncontrolled loss destroys retention:

- New-player protection for the first 8–10 minutes or until the first tower upgrade
- One stolen item maximum per attacker at a time
- A visible ward timer with audio warning
- A short grace period after losing an item
- No stealing from offline players
- Item returns to the owner if the carrier disconnects
- A recovery quest or insurance token after losing a top-tier unit
- Matchmaking/new-player servers where possible

Steal An Egg reportedly gives new players a protected starting area, illustrating how the genre can preserve risk without immediately feeding beginners to veterans.[^54]

## Monetization plan

### Permanent passes

- 2× Mana: 299–399 Robux
- 2× Incubation/Research Speed: 299–399 Robux
- Extra loadout slot or cosmetic tower theme: lower-priced utility/cosmetic pass

Steal An Egg’s reported pass design uses simple acceleration—double income and double egg growth—rather than exclusive content gates.[^62][^54]

### Repeatable products

- 10-minute server luck ritual
- Instant vault reset with a server-wide announcement
- Recovery insurance token
- Short Mana boost
- Mutation reroll that preserves base rarity

Avoid selling an immediate steal or permanent invulnerability; these invalidate the social game. Roblox recommends that purchases provide visible value at natural progression moments and remain fair and transparent.[^61]

### Revenue caution

Concurrent players do not reveal profit. Gross Robux depends on payer conversion, average spend, session geography, Premium payouts, rewarded ads, pricing, regional pricing and platform fees. Public sites currently estimate Steal An Egg anywhere from roughly $2.9 million to $9.4–15 million monthly, which is too wide to treat as factual. Build forecasts from observed payer conversion and revenue per daily active user after launch, not from a competitor’s concurrency.[^2][^1]

## Discovery plan

Do not publish another near-identical green baseplate with the title “Steal a Spell” and expect the trend to carry it. Roblox explicitly advises unique metadata and imagery, accurate descriptions, and an original spin on trends; repetitive titles/images can reduce differentiation.[^63][^59]

Test three honest thumbnail promises:

- **Chase:** Avatar escaping a giant fire guardian while holding a glowing spell
- **Theft:** Rival carrying a legendary spellbook as the tower owner casts Ice
- **Collection:** Tower filled with visually different living spellbooks and one enormous mythic book

Track the first-session funnel:

- Impression → play
- Spawn → understands target
- First vault entry
- First successful return
- First book placed
- First upgrade
- First PvP encounter
- First loss and whether the player continues
- Ten-minute survival
- Next-day return

Roblox identifies play-through rate, first-play bounce, play days and playtime as major Recommendation signals, with intentional co-play and spending signals also relevant. A simple clone can get clicks; a clear, fair first ten minutes are what give it a chance to compound.[^60]

## Legal and security guardrails

Do not use anime characters, YouTuber likenesses, logos, game icons, music, leaked place files, or another developer’s scripts without permission. Roblox lists unauthorized code, media, logos and designs as intellectual-property violations, and its Terms prohibit copying or modifying another creator’s work without written permission or a license.[^64][^65]

If using a kit, use an original, licensed template and verify commercial-use terms. Audit every script. Roblox warns that models can hide runtime-loaded “virus” assets and restricts obfuscated code and remote loading patterns such as `require(assetId)`, `loadstring()`, and runtime asset loading in public Creator Store assets.[^66]

A clean-room implementation based on observable game rules is safer than downloading a purported “uncopylocked” version. The theme, art, UI, economy data and code should all be original or properly licensed.

## Decision

Build **Spell Heist** as a guardian-fetch/base-theft hybrid, not a literal asset swap. Its smallest testable novelty is powerful: **the stolen collectible becomes the ability used in the next robbery**. That single rule connects collection, combat, strategy, status and monetization while remaining simple enough to explain in one sentence.

The immediate prototype milestone is not 36 spells. It is one vault, two abilities, eight plots, a reliable carry/drop system and a five-minute session that makes players voluntarily attempt “one more steal.”
